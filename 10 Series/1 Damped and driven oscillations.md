> [!question] How does a damped oscillator move on its own, and how does it answer a periodic force or any other push?

The three articles follow one equation from the inside out. Without a drive, the natural frequency and the damping rate decide whether the mass rings or creeps back, and their ratio, the quality factor, measures how slowly the energy leaks away. With a periodic drive, the same two numbers fix the amplitude and lag of the steady motion, the height and width of the resonance peak, and the time the oscillator needs to forget how it started. Finally, the response to a single kick turns out to contain all of this: it predicts the motion under any force, and its Fourier transform is the resonance curve.
